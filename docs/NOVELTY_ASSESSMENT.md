# Novelty assessment

## Defensible novelty claim

The proposed contribution is a transparent framework that treats an increment
of heat as a conserved energy quantity with heterogeneous marginal burden and
compares, within one model:

1. same-time spatial allocation;
2. same-location temporal storage;
3. joint spatiotemporal allocation; and
4. progressive physical, environmental, capacity, and transport constraints.

The strongest potential novelty is not the marginal pair-search algorithm, nor
the general observation that heat stress varies geographically. It is the
combination of a marginal-burden field, explicit source-sink accounting,
pathological-optimum diagnostics, and directly comparable spatial versus
temporal thermal-allocation bounds.

## Closest verified precedents

- Thermal resources have been spatially allocated across district-energy
  networks with transportation theory and linear programming.
- Thermal storage is a mature flexibility concept in district heating and
  cooling.
- Anthropogenic heat has been mapped and imposed in regional/global climate
  experiments.
- Conserved-allocation and optimal-transport methods have been used to compare
  and track geophysical fields.

## Claims not currently justified

- "First global heat-redistribution model" is too broad without a systematic
  review of historical waste-heat and climate-engineering literature.
- "Optimal locations to dispose of heat safely" is unsupported because safety
  depends on unresolved local and Earth-system responses.
- "Actionable planetary heat transport" would conflate a theoretical bound
  with engineering feasibility.
- "Reduced mortality" is unsupported by the selected thermal-stress endpoint.

## Novelty gate after results

The manuscript is publishable as an *Applied Energy* research article only if
the constrained comparisons yield robust, interpretable system insight beyond
the trivial result that colder or unpopulated cells accept heat. In particular,
the analysis should establish how short-duration local storage compares with a
matched simultaneous spatial candidate and how exclusion masks and abstract
decision penalties change representative source-sink geography. Any fraction
is event-specific until broader matched-event distributions and technology
calibration are available.
